## Changed

- With `[network.block_download_score_and_hedge]` enabled, the time a legacy peer is expected to
  take for a block now accounts for the block's size and for the peer's record on large blocks.
  It is the peer's round trip, times the round trips a block of that size needs on a new
  connection, times the peer's large-block slowdown factor: a time-weighted average of how its
  earlier blocks over 14,600 serialized bytes compared with that. The prediction is at least
  one round trip. Once the first peer starts returning the requested block and its header hash
  matches, the node uses the message length before the transfer finishes. The decision to send a
  second request then compares the first peer and the other announcers on a block of that size.
  Before, every peer was judged by its round trip whatever the block's size. A block that fits
  one round trip, and any block until its size is known, is handled as before. In
  `delivery_score.jsonl`, `large_block` rows gain `expected_ms` and `slowdown`; in
  `legacy_peer_request.jsonl`, second-request rows gain `decision_block_bytes`, and their
  `first_score_ms` and `score_ms` are the times expected for a block of that size
  ([#2615](https://github.com/zakura-core/zakura/pull/2615)).
