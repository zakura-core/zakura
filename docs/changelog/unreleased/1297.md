Shared serving infrastructure allocates response channels only for jobs with
reserved capacity. Served responses and subscription pages share a queue allowance
that leaves control capacity when the queue has at least two slots. One-slot
queues retain eventual progress as output drains.
Ending publication releases the request count before a peer can observe the
ending and replace its request.
Serving task panics cancel the affected session and connection even while output
is blocked. Other peers remain usable.
Subscription pages reserve queue capacity before committing credit, so closing a
subscription can interrupt both budget and queue waits.
Completion tracking uses one session queue instead of per-request channels.
Records are visible atomically with ending publication, and retired jobs cannot
complete a replacement session's requests.
Production message activation is handled separately.
